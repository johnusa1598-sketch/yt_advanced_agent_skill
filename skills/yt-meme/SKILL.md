# yt-meme

A meme is a joke the audience already knows half of. That is its
power and its danger: use a format the audience recognises and the
joke lands in half a second; force a format that does not fit and
the audience feels managed, which is worse than not being funny.
This skill keeps memes as seasoning - one per Short, two at most
in a long video - never as the video itself.

## Picking a format

Match the format to the beat, not to the trend list:

| Beat in your video | Format that fits | Why it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A fail, then a retry | Reaction shot / caption over the fail | The audience's face is the joke |
| A claim that turns out wrong | Expectation vs reality, two panels | The structure IS the twist |
| A skill that looks easy and is not | "Nobody: ..." / overconfident caption | The gap between claim and reality |
| A sound everyone knows | Sound meme over your own footage | Recognition does the setup for free |
| A series in-joke | A format you already used, repeated | Returning viewers feel in on it |

If no row fits, the video does not need a meme. A forced meme
costs more retention than a flat beat, because the viewer notices
the attempt.

## Punchline placement and timing

- The meme comes **after** the setup beat and **before** the
  payoff, never instead of the payoff. It is a breath, not the
  landing.
- On screen for as long as it takes to read, and no longer: for
  most caption memes that is 1-2 seconds. A meme that lingers
  becomes the video, and the video stops.
- The sound effect lands on the cut (`/yt-sound`). A meme with
  late audio is a joke told twice, badly.
- In a Short, the meme slot is usually the middle turn, not the
  hook and not the loop. The hook earns the watch; the meme
  earns the second watch.

## Caption styling

- Two to four words, bold sans, high contrast, centre or lower
  third. The same style as the text layers (`/yt-post`), because
  a meme in a different font reads as a different video.
- No more than one caption style per video. A Short with three
  fonts is three Shorts fighting.
- If the joke needs more than four words to explain, it is not
  a meme yet. Cut the setup, not the punchline.

## Trend-jacking without cringe

Three tests, same as for any trend (`/yt-trends`): it fits the
series you already make, you add something that is yours, and you
can ship it while it is still moving. One more test just for
memes: would this joke still work if the audience had never seen
the format? If the answer is no, the format is carrying a joke
you do not have. Write the joke first, then find the format.

## Originality, stated plainly

Recreate, do not rip. Do not lift another creator's clip, image,
or caption wholesale and call it a meme. Build the joke from your
own footage, your own character, and your own words, in a format
the audience recognises. Formats are shared; other people's work
is not.

## The meme library method

Keep a log, one line per format: the format, where you saw it,
when you used it, and what happened to retention at that beat
(`retention.py` shows the curve). A format used twice in a month
is a signature; a format used twice in a week is a crutch. The
log is what turns "add a meme" from taste into a decision.

## The gate

Nothing here publishes. This skill writes and you publish. Every
meme slot ends with the format, the beat, the caption, and the
question: **ship it, or change it?**
